# Lesson 6 - Weigh the strands, and say which ones are weak

**Source.** Wharton S, Aronne LJ, Stefanski A, et al. *Orforglipron, an Oral
Small-Molecule GLP-1 Receptor Agonist for Obesity Treatment.* N Engl J Med.
2025;393(18):1796-1806. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2511774 (ATTAIN-1, phase 3)

## Key facts
1. The phase 3 result confirms the final clause of H1: an oral non-peptide
   GLP-1R agonist produces clinically meaningful weight reduction in people with
   obesity. Oral non-peptide agonism is feasible, not merely plausible.
2. An earlier phase 2 study (Wharton S, et al. *Daily Oral GLP-1 Receptor
   Agonist Orforglipron for Adults with Obesity.* N Engl J Med.
   2023;389(10):877-888. doi:10.1056/NEJMoa2302392) established the dose range.
   The phase 2 and phase 3 papers share a first author and are easy to confuse -
   cite the one that matches the claim.
3. The evidence strands are not equally strong. Human genetic support for the
   target and a trial endpoint in humans are strong; the inference from a
   contact map to "undruggable by small molecules at this site" is an argument,
   not a measurement.
4. What this evidence does **not** establish: cardiovascular outcome benefit,
   long-term safety, or durability after discontinuation.
5. The species constraint from lesson 5 limits how much preclinical rodent work
   could ever have de-risked this mechanism - a reason the clinical result
   carries proportionally more weight here.

## Method
1. Take each clause of the hypothesis separately: genetically supported /
   druggable / oral non-peptide agonism feasible.
2. For each, name the strongest piece of evidence and its type (human genetics,
   structure, in vitro pharmacology, clinical outcome).
3. Grade confidence per clause and say what would change your mind.
4. State explicitly what the evidence does not cover.

## Pitfalls
- A single confidence number for a compound hypothesis.
- Promoting mechanism to outcome - weight loss in a trial is not proven
  cardiovascular benefit.
- Reporting high confidence because the conclusion is already well known, rather
  than because this evidence supports it. If you knew the answer in advance, say
  so in the "prior knowledge claimed" section; that is what the control run is
  for.
